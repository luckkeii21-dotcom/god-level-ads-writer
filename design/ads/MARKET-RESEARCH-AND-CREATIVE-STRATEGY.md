## 1. Executive summary

EveryThingAI sells a $997/mo membership (entered via a $20 RizzDial trial) that bundles three things founders can't get under one roof anywhere else: an **AI sales platform** (RizzDial), a **25-person done-with-you team at cost** (Allocard), and **ongoing support** (Skool + office hours). The wedge into cold traffic is **speed-to-lead** — an AI rep that calls every new lead in 60 seconds.

The market is moving in our favor and against us at the same time:

- **Tailwind:** the AI-SDR / AI-voice-agent category is exploding (34.8% CAGR; AI-SDR market $4.27B in 2025 → projected $24.32B by 2034). Buyer awareness of "AI that calls my leads" is now high enough that we don't have to educate from zero.
- **Headwind:** the same boom means the "AI dialer / AI receptionist" claim is now a commodity — GoHighLevel ships an "AI Employee" for **$97/mo**, and raw voice infra (Retell, Bland, Synthflow) runs **$0.07–$0.14/min**. If we lead with "AI calls your leads," we get compared on price and lose.

**The strategic conclusion for creative:** never sell the dialer. Sell the **outcome** (20 booked calls), the **team** ($50K of operators for $997, at cost), and the **identity** (stop being the bottleneck). The 60-second call is the *proof*, not the *pitch*. This is exactly what the locked tagline already encodes — *replace your stack (commodity tools), not your team (the irreplaceable part)*.

## 3. The wedge: speed-to-lead economics (our strongest cold hook)

This is the single most persuasive, most *provable* claim we own. The data is overwhelming and founders feel the pain daily.

- Contacting a lead within **5 minutes → 21× more likely to qualify** vs waiting 30 minutes (MIT / InsideSales).
- Sub-5-minute contact → **32% close rate**, 2.6× higher than 24-hour contact (12%).
- A **1-minute** response drives a **391% conversion lift**.
- Average B2B lead response time is **47 hours**; only **23%** of companies respond within 5 minutes.
- **Up to 73% of leads are never contacted at all**; 35–50% of sales go to the **first** responder.

**Creative implication:** the "47 hours vs 60 seconds" contrast (Creative #2) is a data-driven pattern interrupt that needs no brand trust to land. Lead cold/Top-of-funnel prospecting with it.

## 5. Where we win / where we're exposed (from the Council Verdict — informs creative honesty)

**Win:** at-cost team is structurally real (RizzDial scale revenue subsidizes it), unified persistent memory is a genuine moat, and "we run it on our own business at 100K calls/day" is a credible proof point.

**Exposed (do NOT let creative over-promise these):**
- The **$20 → $997 leap** is a conversion cliff. Creative's job is to sell the **$20 trial + free Build Session**, never to hard-sell $997 in the ad.
- Several moats are **launch-day vaporware** (book unwritten, persistent-memory demo, case studies). → **Creative must avoid claims we can't yet show.** No "see the case study," no "read the book," until they ship.
- **Meta AI-claim risk** — see §7.

**Proof points we *can* use today:** 100K+ outbound calls/day, 25 full-time operators, $50K/mo team value, the at-cost price list ($400 vs $4,000, etc.), 50-seat founding cap, free 15-min Build Session in 72 hrs, 30-day money-back guarantee.

## 7. Meta ad-policy landmines (read before boosting)

Meta escalated enforcement of *misleading claims* and *personal attributes* through 2026 (47 policy updates this year). Two rules matter for us:

1. **Personal attributes — the "you" trap.** Don't use "you/your" to assert a prospect's **negative personal state or protected attribute** (health, finances, etc.). "Are *you* broke / struggling / a failing founder?" → rejected.
   - ✅ Our copy is **business-behavior**, not personal-attribute: *"You're not running your business — it's running you,"* *"You called Tuesday's lead on Thursday."* This is allowed, but keep it about the **work**, never about the person's worth or finances. Avoid "you're losing money/broke."
2. **AI / capability over-claims.** Don't imply the AI fully replaces a human or guarantees outcomes. This is exactly why v3 swapped to *"Add an AI sales rep to your team — free to start"* (additive, honest) instead of "fire your sales team." Keep "AI sales rep," "calls every lead," "helps you" — avoid "autonomous," "replaces your staff," "guaranteed."
3. **Synthetic media labeling.** If any creative uses AI-generated faces/voice (e.g., recorded Aria calls as ad creative — planned in v3), apply Meta's AI-generated label and get the consent SMS on record.

**Net:** the six creatives here are written to be compliance-safe. The riskiest is `ad-03` (the "you" identity angle) — it's within policy because it targets *work habits*, but have a softer variant ready (lead with "The founder bottleneck" instead of "You're…") if the ad account gets flagged.

## 9. Recommended test structure (for Lucky)

- **Stage 1 — angle test (cold):** one ABO/Advantage+ campaign, all six creatives, broad audience, lead/trial objective. Let Meta find the hook. Judge on **cost per $20 trial** (and CPL on the lead form), not CTR alone.
- **Sequencing read:** expect #2 (speed data) and #3 (bottleneck) to win cheapest cold clicks; #1 and #4 to convert warmer; #6 reserved for **retargeting** form-fills/paywall abandoners (Saad's List A).
- **Compliance guardrail:** run the launch through the Sunday-night 5-dummy-lead pipe test first (per offer doc). Keep `ad-03` soft-variant on standby.
- **Iteration:** winning angle → spin 3–4 headline variants of that one creative (the HTML makes this a 60-second change). Don't broaden until an angle clears the capacity-throttle rule (pause spend >80% team utilization).
